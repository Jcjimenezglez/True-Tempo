Task paralysis is a start problem

“Task paralysis” is not laziness with a trendy name. It is sitting in front of work you already agreed to do and feeling physically unable to begin. Keywords Everywhere puts US volume around 6,600 searches a month—people are looking for a way through, not a lecture.

Superfocus is useful here because the product's honest job is starting a timed block, not managing your entire life. Sprint exists for days when Pomodoro feels like a dare.

Why the to-do list makes it worse

A 19-item list is a threat display. Your brain compares “write the whole report” to sitting on the couch and the couch wins. Shrink the unit until it is almost insulting:

  • Open the file
  • Write the ugly first sentence
  • Reply to one email, not the inbox

Put that unit on the Superfocus task line. Nothing else belongs on that line for this timer.

The Sprint protocol

  1. Open Superfocus.
  2. Choose Sprint (short focus, short commitment).
  3. Optional cassette—rain if the room is noisy, lofi if silence is itchy.
  4. When it ends, you have permission to stop. Most people continue. That is the trick: you only owed yourself the Sprint.

If Sprint still does not start, the task is still too big or it is not yours (waiting on someone else, unclear brief). Fix the task, not the timer length.

After you can start: do not jump to Deep Work

People who finally unfreeze often overcorrect into a 90-minute marathon and crash. Use Pomodoro or Flow for the second block. Save Deep Work for days when start energy is already there.

What this is not

Chronic freeze plus despair, missed bills, or inability to do basic self-care is bigger than a focus app. Superfocus will not treat executive dysfunction. It will give you a 15-minute container so “start” has an end time.
